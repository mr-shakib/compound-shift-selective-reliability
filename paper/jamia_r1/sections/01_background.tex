\section*{Background and Significance}

A selective classifier issues a decision only when its confidence clears a
cutoff and refers the remaining cases to a human reader.\cite{chow1970,geifman2017} For chest
radiographs, the per-finding decision thresholds and the abstention cutoff are
normally chosen on data from the developing hospital. If the system is deployed
elsewhere unchanged, the operational question is whether that frozen policy
still delivers the error rate and referral rate it was calibrated for.
Re-fitting thresholds at the new site answers a different question: whether the
method can be re-tuned.

Multimodal models add a second shift. Models that read the clinical indication
or history alongside the image depend on text whose availability and content
reflect local documentation. Institutional and contextual shift therefore
travel together, and an evaluation that varies only one cannot estimate how
they combine.

Each component has been studied separately. Selective classification has been
extended to distribution shift on non-clinical benchmarks.\cite{liang2024}
Failure-detection evaluation practice has been examined, finding simple
confidence baselines hard to beat and proposing the area under the generalised
risk--coverage curve (AUGRC).\cite{jaeger2023,traub2024,bernhardt2022}
Rejection for image-only multi-label chest-radiograph classifiers has been
evaluated within and across datasets, including leave-one-dataset-out
shift.\cite{aperstein2026} Clinical indication
and history improve chest-radiograph classification within a single
source,\cite{jacenkow2022,yang2025} and missing modalities have been treated as
an architectural problem.\cite{wang2025} Chest-radiograph models can lose
performance at hospitals other than the one that developed them,\cite{zech2018}
and cross-dataset failure has been attributed to shift in labels more than in
images,\cite{cohen2020} and report-derived labels are imperfect proxies for
image findings.\cite{jain2021} Using the same labeller, such as
CheXbert,\cite{smit2020} at two sites does not guarantee equal labelling
accuracy or equal label availability.

We found no study that isolates, with predictions and thresholds held fixed,
how label availability and label handling determine transported selective risk.
Image-only rejection has been carried across datasets,\cite{aperstein2026} but
we found none that does so for a multimodal policy while manipulating
pre-diagnostic context under prespecified rules. This paper does both. Its
central result concerns measurement. When per-study error is averaged over
report-derived labels, a difference between hospitals in how often reports
label any target appears as a difference in risk. In our prespecified analysis
code this produced a confirmed cross-site degradation that disappeared once
studies without a labelled target were excluded; an audit after the external
results were seen traced it to the estimator. We give the mechanism in closed
form, quantify it with predictions and thresholds held fixed, and propose
checks that detect it. The significance is methodological and practical:
cross-site selective-risk benchmarks built on report-derived labels can reach
opposite conclusions under defensible analysis choices.
