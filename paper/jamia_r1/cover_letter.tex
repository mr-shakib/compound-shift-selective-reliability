\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit our manuscript, ``Frozen selective prediction for multimodal
chest-radiograph models across two hospitals: a preregistered compound-shift
evaluation with label-dependent cross-site conclusions'', for consideration as
a Research and Applications article.

Selective-prediction systems defer uncertain chest radiographs to a human
reader using thresholds chosen at the developing hospital. Whether such a
frozen policy keeps its error rate and referral rate at another hospital is an
informatics question about deployed artifacts, not only about models. We
evaluated this under a protocol registered before data access, using
MIMIC-CXR-JPG and CheXpert Plus and crossing institutional shift with
controlled changes to pre-diagnostic clinical context.

The paper's central result is methodological and, we believe, of direct
interest to JAMIA readers. An audit carried out after the first external
results showed that the apparent cross-site degradation came from the
selective-risk estimator. Studies whose report labelled none of the target
findings were counted as correct, and such studies were far more common at one
hospital than the other. After correction, the cross-site contrast was not
identified. Defensible choices about uncertain and unmentioned report labels,
together with the training seed, moved it from below zero to well above.
Holding predictions and thresholds fixed, we show which choices drive the sign
and why. We also report what remained stable: frozen thresholds kept coverage
but not per-pathology operating points, and some within-site context effects
were robust. We report the original and corrected estimates side by side, date
every post hoc decision, and provide a blinded adjudication protocol as the
route to a decisive answer.
