\section*{Abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Objective.}
To determine whether a selective-prediction policy frozen at the development
hospital --- per-pathology thresholds and an abstention cutoff --- holds its
operating point when a multimodal chest-radiograph model is deployed where
pre-diagnostic clinical context also differs.

\noindent\textbf{Materials and Methods.}
Preregistered evaluation crossing institutional and context shift. Four models
--- image-only and text-only controls, probability late fusion, and joint
feature fusion --- were developed on MIMIC-CXR-JPG (\num{14766} studies) and
evaluated on CheXpert Plus (\num{132923} informative studies, \num{64709}
patients), thresholds transferred unchanged. At each site, context was
left intact, removed, or replaced with another patient's. The endpoint was
selective five-label Hamming error at \SI{80}{\percent} coverage, with
\SI{95}{\percent} percentile intervals from a patient-clustered bootstrap
(\num{2000} replicates); materiality \num{0.02}.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}
The policy did not transport. With context intact, selective error rose by
\num{0.047} (\SI{95}{\percent} CI \numrange{0.041}{0.053}) for late fusion and
\num{0.038} (\numrange{0.034}{0.043}) for feature fusion. Contrary to
hypothesis, both multimodal models transported \emph{better} than the
image-only control, by \num{0.027} and \num{0.035}. Coverage held across sites
but broke under context intervention, reaching \SI{100}{\percent}. A second
training seed reproduced all seven available verdicts.

\noindent\textbf{Discussion.}
A pre-specified label-source sensitivity reversed every cross-site contrast
while leaving within-site contrasts intact. The image-only control produced
bit-identical external predictions under both label sources yet changed sign,
establishing that the two CheXbert label sets are non-commensurable across
these cohorts (labelling density \SI{44.6}{\percent} versus
\SI{9.1}{\percent}).

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
Cross-site selective-error contrasts are defined only relative to a label
source. Coverage monitoring alone would not have detected the degradation, and
re-calibrating at the target site would have concealed it.
