\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A selective policy frozen at its development hospital did not hold its operating
point at a second institution. The degradation was driven by institution rather
than by context fragility, and adding pre-diagnostic clinical context did not
increase transfer exposure --- both multimodal models transported better than
the image-only control, contrary to our hypothesis and stable across a second
training seed.

Two properties of that failure matter more for practice than its size. It is
invisible to coverage monitoring: every realised coverage stayed inside the
prespecified drift materiality at both sites while selective error rose. And it
is erased by the standard remedy: re-calibrating thresholds at the target site
would have restored the operating point and concealed that the shipped artifact
had never transported. A site that monitored coverage and re-calibrated on
arrival would have observed nothing wrong.

The study also constrains how such comparisons can be made at all. A
prespecified label-source sensitivity reversed every cross-site contrast while
leaving every within-site contrast intact, and the image-only control settles
why: it produced bit-identical external predictions under both label sources
while its transfer gap changed sign. Nothing about the model, the images, or
the intervention differed --- only the labels. The two automatically derived
label sets are not commensurable across these cohorts, differing more than
fourfold in labelling density. Cross-site selective-error contrasts between
these datasets are therefore defined only relative to a label source, and any
benchmark that compares them under automatically derived labels inherits that
constraint.

What generalises is the protocol rather than the effect size. Two sites give a
difference, not a distribution, and the transfer gap measured here should not
be read as the gap to expect elsewhere. What should transfer is the design:
freeze the policy, transfer it unchanged, vary context deliberately at both
sites, and prespecify the label sensitivity --- because on this evidence a
cross-site reliability claim that has not been tested against a second label
source has not yet been tested.
