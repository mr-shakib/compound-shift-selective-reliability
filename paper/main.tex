\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Selective prediction lets a chest-radiograph model defer uncertain cases to a
human reader, and the abstention threshold that fixes its operating point is
chosen once, at the hospital where the model was built. Whether that frozen
policy survives deployment elsewhere is rarely tested, because thresholds are
usually re-fitted at the second site --- which measures whether a method
transfers, not whether the shipped artifact does. A second shift travels with
the first and is usually left implicit: the pre-diagnostic clinical context such
models consume varies across institutions for reasons unrelated to the patient.

We report a preregistered evaluation that crosses these two shifts. Models were
developed and a selective policy calibrated on MIMIC-CXR-JPG, then evaluated on
CheXpert Plus with per-pathology thresholds and abstention cutoffs transferred
unchanged. At each site, context was left intact, removed, or replaced with
another patient's. Four models were compared: image-only and text-only controls,
probability late fusion, and jointly trained feature fusion.

The policy did not transport. With context intact, selective five-label Hamming
error at \SI{80}{\percent} coverage rose by \num{0.047} (\SI{95}{\percent} CI
\numrange{0.041}{0.053}) for late fusion and \num{0.038}
(\numrange{0.034}{0.043}) for feature fusion, each several times the
prespecified materiality threshold. Contrary to our hypothesis, both multimodal
models transported \emph{better} than the image-only control, by \num{0.027} and
\num{0.035} respectively, with intervals excluding zero. Coverage held under
institutional transfer --- feature fusion stayed within \num{0.005} of target at
the unseen hospital --- but broke under context intervention, including a
degenerate case reaching \SI{100}{\percent} coverage.

Selective reliability failed to transport across hospitals, and the failure was
driven by institution rather than by context fragility. Adding clinical context
did not increase transfer risk. Coverage monitoring alone would not have
detected the degradation, and re-calibrating at the target site would have
concealed it.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}

MIMIC-CXR-JPG v2.1.0 and CheXpert Plus are available to credentialed
researchers from PhysioNet and Stanford AIMI respectively. Neither dataset is
redistributed here. The evaluation protocol is versioned and hash-manifested;
the analysis code, the machine-readable protocol registry, and every aggregate
artifact reported in this paper are released, while row-level labels, model
checkpoints and images remain within the protected data environment required by
the data use agreements.

\section*{Preregistration}

The protocol was frozen in a machine-readable registry before any label, model
output, or metric existed, and validated by an automated checker. Every
amendment is dated in an amendment log together with its rationale and its
confirmatory status. At the time of writing all amendments are recorded as
prespecified: none was made after an external result had been inspected.
Changes made after external-result inspection are labelled post-hoc exploratory
by protocol rule.
