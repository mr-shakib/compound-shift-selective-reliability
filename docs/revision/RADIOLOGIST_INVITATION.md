**Subject: Invitation to read chest radiographs for a study on AI reliability
across hospitals (co-authorship)**

Dear Dr [name],

We are evaluating whether an AI system for chest radiographs, calibrated at one
hospital (Boston), keeps its error rate at another (Stanford). Our analysis
found that the answer depends on how labels taken from radiology reports are
handled, so an image-based reading by radiologists is the only way to settle
it. We are inviting [two / three] radiologists to provide that reference.

**What the task involves**
- Reading about 1,430 de-identified chest radiographs (frontal views) in a
  simple local web form. For each study you record whether cardiomegaly,
  edema, consolidation, atelectasis and pleural effusion are present, absent
  or indeterminate, with a confidence from 1 to 5.
- You see only the images. Hospital, report and AI output are hidden, and the
  order is randomised.
- About 1–1.5 minutes per study, roughly 25–35 hours in total, at your own
  pace over [4–6] weeks. Progress is saved after every study.
- A short calibration session on 30 practice images first.
- One senior radiologist resolves disagreements (about 15–25% of findings).

**Before reading: data access (required by the data publishers)**
1. Complete the CITI "Data or Specimens Only Research" course (about 3–4
   hours).
2. Apply for credentialed access on PhysioNet (physionet.org) and sign the
   data use agreement for MIMIC-CXR-JPG. Approval usually takes one to three
   weeks and needs a reference.
3. Accept the Stanford AIMI research use agreement for CheXpert Plus.

Images may not be shared with anyone outside these agreements or uploaded to
any online tool. You would read on a machine prepared by us.

**Credit**
Readers who also review and approve the manuscript meet the ICMJE authorship
criteria and will be co-authors (CRediT: Investigation; Validation;
Writing – review & editing). [Any compensation.]
